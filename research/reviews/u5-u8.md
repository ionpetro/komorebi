# Units 5–8 author review

Reviewed 2026-11-13. This is an AI-assisted author check, not native-speaker or professional certification.

## Scope and content

- u5: clock times, routines, weekdays, frequency and plans.
- u6: arriving at a home, shoes, permission, hospitality and leaving.
- u7: interests, invitations, availability, declining and following up.
- u8: roles, meeting schedules, clarification, borrowing/help and task coordination.

Each of the four units has five lessons. Each lesson contains four word/phrase entries, a sentence build, a grammar card, a culture card and an in-lesson scenario, plus an original dialogue and speaking mission. Total: 20 lessons, 160 lesson items, 80 word/phrase entries and eight separately held-out scenarios. Repeated useful phrases are contextual practice, not a claim of 80 distinct new lexical items.

All scenes, dialogue, questions and explanations are original. The four recurring names in these units are fictional. Japanese uses polite beginner register; kana gives orthographic readings, while は/へ/を particles romanize wa/e/o. Long vowels and small っ are preserved. Sentence builds have 3–7 chunks and distinct distractors. Source provenance distinguishes original communication advice from specific sourced observations.

## Checks completed

- Project content validator on u5–u8: zero errors and warnings.
- All 20 lessons contain the required eight items and four word entries.
- Every dialogue line has Japanese, kana, romaji and English.
- No kanji remains in the kana-reading fields.
- Correct sentence chunks concatenate to the intended Japanese, ignoring punctuation and spaces.
- Every sitting generated across 20 random seeds has bounded length and valid correct choices.
- Assessment prompts use taught patterns. The different clock reading kuji in u5’s second assessment is explicitly glossed because the tested transfer is the approximate-time/wake-up construction, not memorizing an untaught number.

## Editorial decisions and limits

- Cultures are taught through specific situations: an agreed visit, a particular home, a particular team or class. Original practical advice remains labeled general.
- Availability is not treated as agreement; taking a photo is distinguished from sharing it; a softened refusal is not treated as a loophole or mind-reading opportunity.
- No spoken performance is graded. Missions ask the learner to produce speech and self-check; the app cannot establish conversational competence.
- Source observations are narrow; no fluency, JLPT level, official endorsement or native-speaker review is claimed.
