# Japanese curriculum research and provenance

Research checked 2026-11-13. Units 1–4 contain original teaching examples, fictional dialogues and invented practice prices. No copied textbook exercises, borrowed illustrations, native-recording claim, or formal JLPT/CEFR equivalence. Cultural cards carry their source URL; original language examples are explicitly marked general.

## Teaching approach

The Japan Foundation uses practical Can-do objectives and links communication with intercultural understanding. The course design adapts that principle into short missions, scenario decisions, and reflection. Kana practice runs alongside practical speech, with optional reading help. The course's XP, mastery, review intervals and badges are product choices, not prescribed or validated by the sources.

## Editorial decisions

- Culture is contextual and source-backed, with local instructions and personal preferences taking precedence.
- Romaji uses Hepburn-style consonants and macrons for long o/u vowels; kana preserves Japanese orthography. は/へ/を particles are read wa/e/o.
- Dialogue is fictional. The learner is not asked to reveal personal information.
- Ingredient phrases teach communication, not a guarantee that a dish satisfies allergies or other dietary requirements.
- Transport examples are not live route, fare, ticket-product or schedule advice. Prices are invented practice amounts.
- Held-out unit checks use a new setting and previously taught language rather than identical lesson scenes.
- NHK's letter page could not be retrieved in the research environment; it is not cited as verified evidence.
- [Irodori usage FAQ](https://www.irodori.jpf.go.jp/en/faq.html) was checked. This course uses original wording and artwork rather than republishing source materials.
